How the savings happen

  • Avoid the 4–9 pm peak. Grid power costs $0.357/kWh then — more than twice off-peak. Vans are planned into cheaper hours.
  • Rooftop solar first. Free solar from the corporate roof charges vans and fills EV storage; once storage is full, the surplus is pushed to the Corporate Office.
  • EV storage at peak. If the site has storage, it powers the chargers (then the Corporate Office) through the peak. No storage → charging simply waits for off-peak.
  • Spread the load. Keeps the site under its limit (no breaker trips) and the BEV subscription small.

Without the system: vans charge at full power on arrival, no solar or storage, standard thermostat. Only costs that differ are compared. Monthly = 30 nights like this + subscription. Exported solar is not credited.

Each van's plug-in time is drawn from real City of Palo Alto charging sessions (2016–2019, weekday arrivals from 13:00), and its return battery is varied by ±15% (an assumption, not from data). It shows how far the saving moves, and how often every van is ready. A "monthly" figure means 30 nights like that night.

How long the depot's batteries last and what each kWh through EV storage costs in lost battery life. Fade rates come from a public synthetic battery dataset (50,000 readings, 2,600 packs) — a simulated degradation model, not a warranty. The pack is assumed replaced at 80% health with no resale value. The Solar & Storage agent uses this wear cost to decide whether buying cheap grid power to use at peak is still worth it.

Eleven agents run the Corporate Office, grouped under three centralized command agents. Each agent owns one decision; anything that moves stops between vans or reschedules customers waits for a person.
