How the Corporate Office EV solution works

Eleven agents share one goal — the lowest energy bill with every van and semi ready for its route. They report to three command agents — Energy, Fleet and Business — whose conclusions combine into one Corporate Office verdict. Anything that moves stops or reschedules customers waits for a person. watsonx.ai Granite writes the briefs and customer messages.

System at a glance

AI Agent Types in this System

Three distinct AI agent architectures work together across the eleven agents. Each type is implemented in code — not just labelled.

🎯 Goal-Based Agents

Work toward a defined goal state by planning ahead. Actions are chosen because they advance the agent closer to the goal — not just because they look good right now.

  • plan() — depot-engine.js
    Goal: every van reaches its required charge level (need%) before departure. Builds a forward-looking slot schedule over the remaining night.
  • fleetPlanner() — depot-engine.js
    Goal: every van that cannot reach its route charge is either given a lighter route or flagged. Searches for a safe route swap partner and proposes it.
  • chargeDecision() — depot-engine.js
    Goal: the van reaches need% before dep. On each plug-in event, evaluates price, solar and slack to decide charge-now or wait — always tracking the goal.

⚖️ Utility-Based Agents

Score every possible action with a utility function and pick the highest-scoring option. Unlike simple goal-based agents, they can trade off competing goods (cost vs. battery wear vs. grid headroom).

  • utilityScore() inside plan() — depot-engine.js
    Scores each 15-minute charging slot: 0 (solar, free) → 0.001 (stored energy) → grid price ($/kWh). The fill loop always picks the minimum-score slot first — a direct utility maximisation.
  • storageWear() — depot-engine.js
    Computes gridMargin = peak price − grid cost − wear cost per kWh. If margin ≤ 0, the grid top-up is disabled. The agent picks the action with the higher net utility.
  • supervisors() — index.html
    Aggregates Energy, Fleet and Business statuses into a worst-case depot verdict (GO / WATCH / ACTION NEEDED) by taking the maximum severity across all three domains.

🧠 Learning Agents

Improve behaviour over time by observing outcomes across multiple sessions. The loop is: observe past results → learn a correction → act differently tonight.

  • learningAdjust() — index.html
    Reads per-van history (up to 10 nights) from SQLite via loadTrend(). Detects two patterns and derives a charge-target bump:
    — Slow-charger compensation: if a van averaged <85% of requested power, the target is raised by up to +10% so the van still finishes in time.
    — Deep-discharge guard: if a van arrived below 16% SoC on the majority of recorded nights, +5% is added as a safety buffer.
  • plan() consumes cfg.learningNeeds — depot-engine.js
    Before scheduling each van, applies the bump from learningAdjust(), capped at 100%. The 🧠 icon appears in the Battery health card on the Agents tab when a target was raised.
  • healthText() / loadTrend() — index.html
    The observation layer: loads multi-night vehicle records and surfaces trends (arrival SoC, cost per stop, slow-charge ratio) that feed the learning logic.

How it works

  • Corporate Office (13:00–06:00, or a full day from 06:00): vans return with the battery left from their route. The power agent plans each van's charge into the cheapest hours — rooftop solar, then super off-peak, then off-peak — and avoids the 4–9 pm peak unless a van would miss its departure. It spreads load to stay under the site limit and keep the BEV subscription small.
  • Rooftop solar and EV storage (only if the site has them — the setup asks): solar charges vans and fills storage; once storage is full, the surplus goes to the Corporate Office. Storage powers the vans' required charging through the peak, then the Corporate Office, and keeps a reserve for outages. During an outage, solar and storage run the Corporate Office at any hour.
  • Solar & Storage agent: owns solar routing — vans first, then EV storage, then the Corporate Office — and decides when to hold storage for the peak or an outage. When the grid returns it refills storage from solar plus the grid.
  • Command agents (the centralized layer): Energy Command (power, solar & storage, tariff, outage resilience, demand response), Fleet Command (fleet planner, dispatch, battery health, charger faults) and Business Command (customer communications, reporting). Each reads its agents and reaches one conclusion — GO, WATCH or ACTION NEEDED. The worst of the three is the Corporate Office verdict shown on the Agents tab and in the morning brief.
  • Specialist agents: Outage resilience estimates runtime on storage + solar and ranks vans by urgency; Demand response estimates the flexibility the site could offer at peak and measures the grid power actually avoided (simulated, no program rates); Battery health tracks cost per van and per stop, power delivered vs requested, and trends across saved nights from SQLite; Charger faults opens a maintenance ticket (stored in SQLite), tracks downtime and tells the planner; Customer communications drafts heads-ups, escalates when a late van is within 2 hours of departure, and records replies the dispatcher logs; Reporting writes the daily and monthly owner report and queues it — nothing is emailed until a connector is set up.
  • Fleet planner agent: ranks vans by urgency and, if a van cannot reach its route charge, proposes swapping routes with a lighter-route van. It only proposes; the dispatcher approves.
  • Tariff agent: checks the BEV subscription size and compares the Corporate Office meter rate with alternatives. It recommends; it never switches a rate.
  • HVAC: when the Corporate Office thermostat triggers, the agent stops HVAC once it's comfortable and re-triggers it only 5% below the actual trigger temperature, freeing 30 kW for chargers.
  • Dispatch: starts from each van's overnight charge. On traffic or an accident it chooses waiting or rerouting itself. Moving stops to another van or rescheduling customers waits for the dispatcher's approval.
